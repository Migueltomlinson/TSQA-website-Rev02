The Health and Safety at Work Amendment Act 2026 was passed on 1 July 2026. Most of it starts on 1 April 2027. That feels like a long way off, but if you run a small business or contract to bigger ones, there is real work to do before then. Doing nothing is the most expensive option.

## What is changing

The biggest shift is a new focus on **critical risks**. A critical risk is one linked to the hazards listed in the new Schedule 1A of the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015 (HSWA), or to any hazard likely to cause death, a notifiable injury or illness, a notifiable incident, or a listed occupational disease. "Likely" here refers to the chance of serious harm if an incident happens, not how often the incident occurs. Psychosocial hazards can qualify too.

How this applies depends on your size:

- **Small PCBUs** (fewer than 20 workers for at least nine months of a financial year) must manage critical risks first.
- **Larger PCBUs** must still manage all risks, but put critical risks at the front of the queue for time, money and resources.

Other changes include narrower duties for officers (governance, oversight and checking that controls work), compliance with another Act such as the Building Act 2004 counting as compliance with HSWA for the same risk, and following an approved code of practice being treated as meeting your duty for the risk it covers.

Until 1 April 2027, you must comply with HSWA as it stands today.

## What this does not mean

Being a small business does not mean being exempt. You still owe workers the basics: first aid, washing facilities, lighting, worker engagement and notifying WorkSafe of notifiable events. And a narrower legal duty does not shrink the actual risk. Your insurer, your ACC levy and your clients will still care about incidents.

Contractors should note that the worker count is broad. Contractors and subcontractors can count towards the 20-worker threshold, so some businesses that think of themselves as small may not be.

Also, larger clients can still require you to meet higher standards by contract. Pre-qualification schemes such as SiteWise, IMPAC, SHE and Tōtika, and ISO 45001, do not disappear because the Act changed. If a main contractor asks for evidence of your systems, "the law only requires critical risks now" will not win you the job.

## Six steps to take before April

1. **Count your workers.** Use the HSWA definition, which includes contractors and subcontractors. Record how you reached your answer and review it annually.
2. **List your critical risks.** Walk through your work and note every hazard that could kill or seriously injure someone. Write down why each one qualifies. Do not leave this in your head.
3. **Check your controls actually work.** For each critical risk, ask what stops it hurting someone and how you know that control is used on site. This is where officers get caught out. The High Court's recent *Gibson v Maritime New Zealand* decision reinforces that officers must test whether critical controls work in practice.
4. **Look for overlapping rules and codes.** If another Act or an approved code already covers a risk, you may be able to rely on it. Identify which ones apply to your trade.
5. **Review your contracts.** Check what your clients expect from you and what you expect from your subcontractors on information sharing, standards and incident reporting. Contracts do not shift your legal duties, but unclear ones cause arguments after an incident.
6. **Brief your leadership.** If you are an owner, director or manager, you are an officer. Be able to say what your critical risks are, what resources you have put against them, and how you verify they are working.

## The cost of waiting

The election is due before April 2027, so details could still move. That is a reason to build a flexible, risk-based system now, not a reason to wait. A clear critical risk register and working controls help under the current law, help under the new one, and help when a client or pre-qualification assessor asks to see your system. Leaving it until March means doing it in a rush, under pressure, probably with an incident or a tender deadline looming.

WorkSafe says more detailed guidance will be released before 1 April 2027, so keep an eye on it.
