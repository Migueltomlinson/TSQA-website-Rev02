For many New Zealand contractors, health and safety can feel like a moving target. New clients ask for different paperwork, pre-qualification schemes want evidence you are not sure you have, and the day job still has to get done. The good news is that a genuinely safe business and a work-ready one are built on the same foundations. Get the basics right and most of the rest follows.

Here are the core areas we help contractors get in order.

## 1. Know your risks and write them down

Under the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, every business has a duty to manage the risks that come with its work. That starts with identifying the hazards specific to your operations, deciding how serious each one is, and setting out the controls you will use to manage it.

A [risk assessment](/health-safety/risk-management/) does not need to be long or complicated, but it does need to be real and used on site. It is the document most clients and assessors will look for first.

## 2. Have a health and safety system people actually use

A safety management system is simply the set of policies, processes, and records you use to manage safety consistently. The test of a good system is not how thick the folder is. It is whether your team can pick it up and use it.

If you want your system built to an internationally recognised benchmark, it can be structured to the [ISO 45001 framework](/health-safety/safety-systems/). Certification itself is decided by an accredited certification body, but a well built system puts you in a strong position either way.

## 3. Keep your training and induction records current

Clients and pre-qualification schemes want to see that the people doing the work are competent and inducted. Keep simple, dated records of inductions, toolbox talks, and any tickets or qualifications your crew holds. Records that are current and easy to find will save you time on every tender.

## 4. Have a plan for when something goes wrong

Two things matter here: knowing what to do in an emergency, and learning from incidents when they happen. Clear emergency response plans, tested with the occasional drill, help your team respond with confidence. And when something does go wrong, a straightforward [incident investigation](/health-safety/incident-culture/) that looks for the underlying cause is what stops it happening again.

## 5. Get pre-qualified for the work you want

If you are chasing work with councils, principals, or main contractors, you will likely need a recognised pre-qualification such as IMPAC PREQUAL, SiteWise, SHE Pre-Qual, or Totika. The simplest approach is to ask your target clients which scheme they accept, then build your evidence to suit. Our guide to [contractor pre-qualification](/pre-qualification/) walks through each scheme and the questions contractors ask most.

## Where to start

If this feels like a lot, start with the risk assessment and a basic system, then build out records and pre-qualification as you go. You do not have to do it all at once, and you do not have to do it alone.
