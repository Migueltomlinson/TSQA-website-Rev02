There is a quiet trap sitting between two changes that happened this year, and a lot of small contractors are going to walk straight into it.

The first change made headlines. The Health and Safety at Work Amendment Act 2026 passed in July, with the main changes taking effect on 1 April 2027. It creates a new category of "small PCBU" for businesses with fewer than 20 workers, and it narrows what those businesses have to manage down to critical risks, the things genuinely capable of killing or seriously harming someone. The message most owners took from the news coverage was simple: less paperwork is coming.

The second change made almost no noise at all. SiteWise refreshed its assessment questionnaire in 2026. The current version runs to 13 questions worth 180 points, and in several places it asks for more evidence than the old one, not less.

Those two things are moving in opposite directions, and only one of them decides whether you win work next year.

## What the new SiteWise assessment actually asks for

A few details worth knowing before your next reassessment.

Evidence has to be less than 12 months old. Not "we did that a while back". Dated, current records.

Question 7, hazard and risk management, is the single biggest scoring question at 25 points, and it now requires a **master** risk register covering every area where work happens. Offices, workshops, warehouses, vehicles, mobile plant, the lot. SiteWise has stated plainly that site or location specific registers will no longer be awarded full marks. If your risk register is a stack of site-specific documents, you have just lost points you used to get.

Question 12 covers mental health and psychosocial risk. It is only worth 5 points, but it needs a policy statement signed by an officer of your company within the last 24 months. If you have never written one, that is a zero.

Question 4, incidents and near misses, is worth 20 points and wants an incident register with *regular* entries across the full 12 months, plus a completed investigation showing root cause, plus proof you communicated the outcome to your workers. A blank register with no evidence of review does not pass.

Question 5 wants proof you pre-qualified a subcontractor **and** physically inspected their work in the last 12 months.

None of that goes away on 1 April 2027.

## Why the gap matters

The law sets the floor for what WorkSafe can prosecute you for. Your clients set the bar for who gets on site. Those are two different bars, and the commercial one is higher.

Main contractors, councils, port companies and large asset owners across the Bay of Plenty, Waikato, Auckland and Taranaki use SiteWise, Tōtika, IMPAC PREQUAL and SHE Pre-Qual to decide who they will engage. They are not going to relax their procurement standards because Parliament narrowed the statutory duties of a 12-person firm. If anything, a narrower legal floor makes independent pre-qualification a *more* important filter for them, not less.

So the risk is not that a smaller contractor gets prosecuted for something outside critical risk. The risk is that they hear "reform means less compliance", quietly stop maintaining the register and the meeting records and the training matrix, and then drop a grade at their next annual reassessment. The invoice for that arrives as work you did not get invited to tender for. Nobody sends you a letter explaining why.

## What to do in the next month

Pull up your last SiteWise report and look at the questions where you lost points. That is your list, and it is usually short.

Consolidate your risk registers into one master register that covers every workplace, with initial risk, controls, residual risk, and a dated review. This is the highest value hour you can spend, because Question 7 is worth 25 points on its own.

Check your dates. Policy statement signed within 24 months, procedures reviewed within 24 months, everything else within 12. Old evidence scores the same as no evidence.

Write the psychosocial risk statement. It can sit inside your existing health and safety policy. Get it signed.

Then look ahead. Between now and April 2027, identify your genuinely critical risks, the ones that could kill or permanently injure someone, and make sure you can show those controls operating with dates and names. That work serves both masters. It is what the amended Act will focus on, and it is what a pre-qualification assessor wants to see anyway.

The reform is not an excuse to do less. It is a chance to stop spreading effort thinly across everything and put it where it counts, while keeping the evidence trail your clients require.
