The Health and Safety at Work Amendment Act 2026 comes into force on 1 April 2027. That is about four and a half months away. If you run a small business or a contracting crew, the headline change is simple: you will be expected to know your critical risks and manage them first.

## What is changing

The Amendment Bill passed in July 2026 and the changes take effect on 1 April 2027. WorkSafe has said more guidance will be released before then, so some details are still being finalised. Here is what we know so far.

**Critical risks come first.** Critical risks are linked to the hazards listed in Schedule 1A of the Health and Safety at Work Act 2015, or to hazards likely to cause death, a notifiable injury or illness, or a listed occupational disease. Think working at height, vehicles and mobile plant, hazardous substances, confined spaces, electrical work and manual handling that causes serious harm.

**Size matters.** Small PCBUs (businesses with fewer than 20 workers for at least nine months of the financial year) must manage critical risks ahead of other risks. Larger PCBUs must manage both critical and other risks, with critical risks given the highest priority. Your baseline duties, such as facilities and worker support, do not disappear.

**Officer duties are narrower.** Directors and senior managers are now judged on a governance role: understand the risks, make sure resources and processes exist, and check they are actually used. For owner-operators who also work on the tools, this separation is worth understanding.

**Codes of practice get a stronger standing.** Following an approved code of practice will count as meeting your duties for that risk. Industry groups can also develop codes for approval.

This is a summary, not legal advice. Check the final guidance when WorkSafe releases it.

## Why this matters for your business

Many SME owners hear "law change" and wait to see what happens. That is understandable, but waiting has a cost. The first time your critical risks are tested will not be a quiet audit. It will be after an incident, or when a client asks to see your risk register before awarding a contract.

Pre-qualification schemes such as SiteWise, SHE Pre-Qual, IMPAC and Tōtika already ask how you identify and control your highest-risk work. A clear critical risk register makes those applications easier and helps you win work. Insurers and principal contractors are also asking sharper questions. The cost of doing nothing is avoidable: fines, ACC and insurance consequences, lost tenders and the harm to your people.

## Five steps to take before April

**1. List your critical risks.** Walk through a typical week of work. Write down every task where a mistake could kill or seriously injure someone. Use the Schedule 1A hazards as a prompt. Most small businesses end up with five to ten.

**2. Check your controls are real.** For each critical risk, ask what stops someone getting hurt. Is it a physical control, a procedure, or just "we tell the new guys"? A control that depends on someone remembering is weak. Ask the people doing the job.

**3. Put critical risks at the top.** If your register is a long list where a trip hazard sits next to a crush hazard, reorder it. Your toolbox talks, inductions and audits should start with the critical risks.

**4. Sort out your subcontractors.** Look at who works under you and who you work under. Co-operation duties are tightening, so agree who controls which critical risk on shared sites and put it in writing.

**5. Record who is checking.** If you are an owner or director, keep simple evidence that you are verifying the controls work: a monthly site visit note, a review of incidents and near misses, a short report to yourself or your board. That is the governance role in practice.

## Where quality fits

Critical risk control is a quality problem as much as a safety one. A procedure that nobody follows is a process failure. The same discipline that keeps your work defect-free, such as clear steps, trained people and checking the result, is what keeps your critical controls working. Fix it once and it serves both.
